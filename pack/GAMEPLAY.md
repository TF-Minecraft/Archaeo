# Archaeo — gameplay guide

## 1. Find a ruin

Hold the **tracker**. Pulses get faster as you near a ruin nobody has claimed
yet. If you already planted a camp there, the tracker stays quiet. If the
beeps fade, you walked the wrong way.

When you are close enough, chat tells you to prospect the ground.

## 2. Confirm it (prospect)

Use the **prospecting kit** on shovel-dug earth: dirt, grass, sand, gravel,
clay, mud. Furniture and stone do not count.

Hold still for each sample. Walking off cancels it. Spread the samples out.
Weak traces mean keep going until chat **confirms** the site. That still does
not make it yours.

## 3. Plant the camp

Use the **establishment kit** only after confirmation. Aim at a **neighbour
chunk**, not the ruin itself. A ghost of the camp follows your look; turn
until it sits cleanly, then confirm.

You become **director**. The tracker will never sing for this ruin again.

The tents sit beside the dig, not on it. The **prism** is the ruin ground and
the layers under it. Putting up camp does not spend the work day.

Use the **board** at camp for the dossier, who may work, the finds list, and
**Show limits** — glowing edges of the cut, for you only, and only for a
moment.

If you already run as many camps as you are allowed, close one before you
claim another.

## 4. Dig (hear, then release)

Inside the dig site, hold **left-click** with an **excavation tool**. The
block does not crack like vanilla mining. Listen:

| Cue | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Soft clings, then a louder ready chime | Empty fill. **Release on the ready chime** to lift cubes. |
| Release too soon | Nothing leaves. The next hold starts clean. |
| Release too late | More cubes leave. You have less control. |

The HUD names the **stratum** you are in, and may show how many cuts you have
left today.

A light tool takes a narrow shaft. A heavier one takes a wider bite, and a
late release always takes more than a timely one. Cues arrive at the speed
that tool would mine that block.

When empty fill comes out, chat and the HUD can show **traces** — how much of
each material is still touching the hole. Use that to slow down.

If you ignore a find cling and punch through the piece, it is hurt. A
destroyed cell is gone for good. A normal pick in the cut can wreck a buried
find the same way, even before anyone has camped.

## 5. Brush the find out

A find is a **shape** in the dirt, not a loot block. When every remaining
cube of that shape has air on a face, they drip. **Hold right-click** with
the **brush** on a dripping cube. Look away to pause that cube; look back to
continue.

Keep dusting until the piece comes free — one item, with how well it survived
and where it came from. The pick never drops it. If nothing is left of it,
nothing drops.

The label on the item (intact, worn, crumbling, …) is how it reads. What the
ground already did to it was decided before you arrived. Careful digging
cannot turn a rotten piece pristine.

## 6. Clean, sketch, register

Take the piece to the **cabinet**. Right-click with it in hand. Sneak if you
only wanted the vanilla use of that block.

- dirty → wipe in the lab window (each stain names the tool that lifts it)
- cleaned, no drawing yet → register
- already filed → open a reading

Empty-handed clicks do not open that window.

**Field sheet** and **pencil**: click the sheet onto the pencil in the
inventory, or hold the sheet and use the pencil in the other hand. Sign the
sketch, then register at the cabinet with the find in hand.

A **workshop** (the Archeology Table, if you have one) is for making all
plugin-related tools.

## 7. Museum

Build any room you like. **Shift + right-click** a recovered find on a frame,
lectern, shelf, armor stand, or a case that accepts plaques. You get the same
reading you would from the camp board. Click without shift is ordinary
Minecraft — hang, rotate, take. Empty slots and junk items do nothing extra.

## 8. When the site is finished

When every find is out or destroyed, the excavation **exhausts**. Closing the
camp from the board is how you leave it and free yourself for another ruin.

You receive a **field book**. Right-click it later to keep reading the
excavation record — the same dossier and finds you had at the board — even
though the tents may be gone.
